# 事实核查（十二章版新增条目）

核查方式：三组研究员独立检索，再由一名质疑者逐条尝试推翻。下表列出最终采用的稳妥措辞与来源。

## A1（初判：correct；质疑后维持）
- 稳妥措辞：《周易·系辞下》记载：“上古结绳而治，后世圣人易之以书契。”《庄子·胠箧》亦称上古之时“民结绳而用之”。
- 核查发现：The Wikisource text of 《周易·系辞下》 (chapter 2, last paragraph) reads: "上古結繩而治，後世聖人易之以書契，百官以治，萬民以察，蓋取諸夬。" The quoted wording matches exactly. The Wikisource text of 《庄子·胠篋》 reads: "昔者容成氏、大庭氏……伏戲氏、神農氏，當是時也，民結繩而用之，甘其食，美其服，樂其俗，安其居……" So the exact phrase is "民结绳而用之". The phrase "结绳而用之" also appears in 《老子》第八十章 ("使人复结绳而用之"), so it is not unique to Zhuangzi.
- 质疑意见：No error found. 《系辞下》 reads '上古結繩而治，後世聖人易之以書契' and 《胠篋》 reads '當是時也，民結繩而用之'. The Zhuangzi passage is set in the age of legendary rulers such as 容成氏 and 神農氏, so '上古之时' is a fair paraphrase. Minor note: 《系辞》 belongs to the 《易传》, but '《周易·系辞下》' is the standard way to cite it.
- 来源：https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周易/繫辭下；https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/胠篋；https://ctext.org/book-of-changes/xi-ci-xia/ens

## A2（初判：correct；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：印加结绳以十进制记数，用单结、长结和“8”字结表示数字。据2021年的统计，已知存世的结绳近一千四百件；记录非数字信息的结绳，至今大多未能破译。
- 核查发现：Knots: Locke (1912) showed that khipus record numbers in a base-10 positional system. Clusters of single (overhand) knots stand for tens and higher places. Long knots of 2–9 turns stand for 2–9 in the units place, a figure-eight knot stands for 1, and an empty position stands for 0. A rarer 'EE' knot also exists, and Hyland (2024) has proposed revisions to Locke's typology. Dates: Britannica dates Pachacuti's reign, the start of imperial expansion, to 1438–1471. Pizarro captured Atahuallpa in November 1532, and Atahuallpa was executed in 1533. Wikipedia gives the empire as 1438–1533 and says khipus flourished in the Andes from c. 1100 to 1532. So 'c. 1400–1532' is close, but '15世纪至1532年' is more precise. Survivors: Medrano's 2021 census found at least 1,386 khipus in 143 museums and private collections in more than 20 countries. He wrote 'almost 1,400' in 2023. A 2024 article in The Conversation (Karen Thompson, Univ. of Melbourne) says about 1,600 remain. Older figures of about 600 (Urton 2003) and about 1,000 (Harvard 2017) are out of date. Decipherment: the numerical function is understood. Non-numerical or possibly narrative khipus are still undeciphered, and proposed readings are disputed. The Aschers estimated that about 80% of khipus are numerical. Best figure to use: about 1,400 (census), with 'more than 1,400' or 'about 1,400–1,600' also defensible.
- 质疑意见：The count needs an anchor. Medrano's 2021 census found 'at least 1,386', and Wikipedia calls this 'just under 1,400'. But a 2024 University of Melbourne / The Conversation article says 'about 1,600 remain', so a bare '约一千四百件' can look contradicted or out of date. Saying '近一千四百件' with the year of the census is safer. The phrase '其中非数字信息' is also loose, because it reads as if the 1,400 objects contain the information. The knot description (single, long and figure-eight knots, base 10) is accurate.
- 来源：https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quipu；https://revista.drclas.harvard.edu/collecting-history；https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/news/94927-the-incas-used-mysterious-stringy-objects-called-%E2%80%98khipus%E2%80%99-to-record-data.-we-just-got-a-step-closer-to-understanding-them；https://utpress.utexas.edu/9780292723672/narrative-threads/；https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pachacuti-Inca-Yupanqui；https://www.britannica.com/biography/Francisco-Pizarro

## A3（初判：correct；质疑后维持）
- 稳妥措辞：负责制作与解读结绳的专职人员，被称为“结绳官”（khipukamayuq）。
- 核查发现：The British Museum collection entry (Am1907,0319.286) calls the Inca administrators who handled khipus 'khipucamayuq' and says they could read the stored data. Wikipedia glosses the Quechua term khipu kamayuq as 'khipu-authority'. These were the accountants of Tawantinsuyu: they made and read the cords, did calculations, kept census records and tracked mit'a labour tax. Thompson (2024) describes them as specially trained makers who chose cord colour, spin, ply and knot structure deliberately.
- 质疑意见：Acceptable. Wikipedia describes khipu kamayuq as the officials who read quipus, glossed 'khipu-authority' or 'khipu specialist', and the British Museum says they could read the stored data. '结绳官' is a Chinese rendering, not a fixed standard term, but putting it in quotation marks with the original word next to it makes that clear. The term can also be written as two words, khipu kamayuq.

## A4（初判：needs_correction；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：1583年，第三次利马宗教会议颁令，要求各地主教将原住民用于“迷信”仪式的结绳全部收缴。
- 核查发现：The Third Council of Lima was a church council, not a civil colonial authority. Archbishop Toribio de Mogrovejo convened it, and it met from 15 August 1582 to 18 October 1583. Its decree, quoted by PUCP from the acts, reads: 'procuren con diligencia los obispos que todos los memoriales o quipos que sirven para su superstición, se les quiten totalmente a los yndios'. Lisi's (1990) English translation reads: 'the bishops must be sure to destroy them completely, for they are pernicious instruments'. The decree therefore targeted khipus tied to 'superstition' (non-Christian ritual), not all khipus. The same council's materials encouraged khipus for confession, and a 1585 sermon tells penitents to 'make a quipo' of their sins. Some secondary sources, including Wikipedia's Quipu article, say the khipus were 'ordered burned', but the Spanish wording of the acts is about removal or destruction, not fire. The script should not say 'all khipus' or 'burned'.
- 质疑意见：'销毁' (destroy) goes beyond the decree. Article 37 of the acts promulgated in 1583, as quoted by PUCP from Bartra 1982, says the bishops should see that 'todos los memoriales o quipos que sirven para su superstición, se les quiten totalmente a los yndios'. That orders the khipus taken away entirely; it does not say destroy or burn. Destruction and burning come from secondary accounts, and Brokaw questions the traditional story of a universal ban. Also, the decree covers khipus 'used for their superstition', not khipus in general 'regarded as superstitious objects'.

## A5（初判：needs_correction；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：目前已知年代最早且无争议的结绳实物，出自安第斯的瓦里文化，经碳十四测定，距今已有一千多年。
- 核查发现：Urton (Antiquity 88(339), 2014) attributes a group of eight khipus to the Wari culture of the later Middle Horizon (AD 600–1000) and reports AMS dates for four of them. Cherkinsky & Urton (2014, Radiocarbon chronology of Andean khipus) report that three Wari khipus date to AD 780–1024. So the radiocarbon dates fall in roughly the 8th–11th century, not the 7th–10th; 'AD 600–1000' is the period of their context. Earlier claims, such as a proto-khipu from Caral (c. 3000–1800 BCE), have not been evaluated or are disputed, and should not be used. AD 780–1024 is about 1,000–1,250 years before 2026, so 'more than 1,000 years old' (一千多年) is the safe figure. '1,300 years' is not supported.
- 质疑意见：'现存最古老的结绳' states as fact something that has caveats. Wikipedia calls the Wari khipus (Middle Horizon, c. 600–1000 CE) the 'first undisputed evidence'. Older candidates exist: a claimed proto-khipu from Caral (c. 3000–1800 BCE) that has not been evaluated, and the Cerrillos wrapped batons (c. 350–200 BCE). The radiocarbon dates themselves are fine: Cherkinsky & Urton 2014 give AD 780–1024 for three Wari khipus, which is about 1,000–1,250 years ago, so '一千多年' holds. Only the superlative needs a hedge.
- 来源：https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/from-middle-horizon-cordkeeping-to-the-rise-of-inka-khipus-in-the-central-andes/04964C31803194A5EA0F1565617F00C9；https://www.pagepress.org/journals/arc/article/view/arc.2014.5260；https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quipu

## A6（初判：correct；质疑后维持）
- 稳妥措辞：约公元前3300年，乌鲁克出现原始楔形文字，主要用于记录行政账目。公元前612年尼尼微陷落，据信大火使亚述巴尼拔图书馆的部分泥板受热变硬。大英博物馆收藏的该馆泥板及残片超过三万件。
- 核查发现：Proto-cuneiform: CDLI dates the Uruk IV phase to about 3350–3200 BC and Uruk III (Jemdet Nasr) to about 3200–3000 BC. About 5,000 tablets are known, roughly 85% administrative and 15% lexical lists. A CDLI/Unicode count gives 4,167 administrative and 703 lexical texts. The Met says early writing was used primarily to record economic information. 'About 3300 BCE, mostly administrative accounts' is accurate. Nineveh fell in 612 BCE to a coalition of Babylonians, Medes and Scythians. On the fire, Wikipedia says it 'is believed' that the burning of the palace left the tablets 'partially baked', which helped preserve them. This is an inference, and some library tablets may already have been fired when made, so the claim should be hedged. The British Museum says over 30,000 clay tablets and fragments were found at Nineveh, and its database counts 30,943 'tablets' for the Nineveh library. These probably represent only about 10,000 original texts, so the figure counts tablets and fragments, not whole texts.
- 质疑意见：Accurate as hedged. Uruk IV is about 3350–3200 BCE, and Wikipedia puts the first unequivocal writing at about 3300 BC. Most proto-cuneiform tablets are administrative. Nineveh fell in 612 BCE. The baking claim is already hedged with '据信'; this matters because the British Museum project stresses that the library was sifted and deliberately smashed, and some scholars say library tablets were fired when made. The British Museum calls the 'Library of Ashurbanipal' 'a collection of over 30,000 clay tablets and fragments', and its database counts 30,943, so '泥板及残片超过三万件' is supported. Keep the word '残片': these pieces represent only about 10,000 original texts.
- 来源：https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-cuneiform；https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/periods；https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2019/19284-proto-cuneiform.pdf；https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-origins-of-writing；https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Ashurbanipal；https://www.britishmuseum.org/research/projects/what-was-ashurbanipals-library；https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/library-fit-king；https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform

## A7（初判：needs_correction；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：最迟至12世纪中叶，造纸术已经由阿拉伯世界传入伊比利亚半岛，沙蒂瓦（又译哈蒂瓦）成为欧洲最早有文献记载的造纸中心之一。
- 核查发现：Xàtiva (Játiva) near Valencia, then under Muslim rule, is usually cited as producing paper by about 1150–1151. Al-Idrisi, writing about 1150, praised Xàtiva paper as unmatched and said it was exported east and west. Two problems with the claim: (1) 'paper mill' is misleading. A survey of milling in al-Andalus found no water-powered paper mills, and the first clear water-powered mill at Xàtiva is a 1282 decree of Peter III of Aragon. (2) Production at Xàtiva is not documented for the 11th century. The paper in the Missal of Silos (before 1080, the oldest known European paper manuscript) is only believed to come from al-Andalus. Italy's Fabriano (13th century) and Nuremberg (Stromer, 1390, the first permanent paper mill north of the Alps) came later. Safe wording: by the 12th century, papermaking had reached al-Andalus via the Islamic world, and Xàtiva was one of Europe's earliest papermaking centres.
- 质疑意见：Two problems. (1) '约12世纪中叶……传入' makes the mid-12th century the date papermaking arrived. That is only when Xàtiva production is first documented (al-Idrisi, c. 1150). The paper in the Missal of Silos, made before 1080/1089, is believed to come from al-Andalus, so papermaking probably arrived earlier. 'By the mid-12th century at the latest' is the defensible form. (2) '萨蒂瓦' is not a standard Chinese name. Chinese Wikipedia uses 沙蒂瓦 (from Valencian Xàtiva) or 哈蒂瓦 (from Spanish Játiva). 'Earliest documented' is also safer than 'earliest', because the 1056/1074 founding dates are unsupported legend.
- 来源：https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_mill；https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=2546；https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missal_of_Silos；https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=5033；https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/沙蒂瓦

## A8（初判：correct；质疑后维持）
- 稳妥措辞：唐咸通九年（868年）刊印的《金刚经》，是现存最早有明确纪年的印刷书籍。北宋庆历年间，毕昇发明泥活字，事见沈括《梦溪笔谈》，实物未能留存。约1455年，古腾堡印成四十二行《圣经》，现存约四十九部（含残本）。至1500年，欧洲印刷书籍已达约三万种版本。
- 核查发现：Diamond Sutra: the colophon dates it to the 15th day of the 4th month of Xiantong 9 (咸通九年四月十五日), i.e. 11 May 868, and says Wang Jie had it made for his parents. It is in the British Library (Or.8210/P.2), is described as the world's earliest dated printed book, and is a woodblock print. Undated block printing is older, so the precise phrase is '现存最早有明确纪年的印刷品'. Bi Sheng: 《梦溪笔谈》卷十八 says '慶曆中，有布衣畢昇，又為活版。其法用膠泥刻字……火燒令堅。' The Qingli era ran 1041–1048. Shen Kuo says that after Bi Sheng died, the type passed to Shen's cousins ('昇死，其印為余群從所得，至今保藏'), but none of the original type survives. Gutenberg Bible: the first finished copies were available in 1454 or 1455, and 158–180 copies were printed. Wikipedia and the Gutenberg Museum count 49 surviving copies at least in substantial part (about 21 complete). The Morgan says 50 and the Free Library 48, so 'about 49' is the right wording. Incunabula: the ISTC (CERL, founded by the British Library) listed 30,596 editions as of October 2024, so 'about 30,000 editions' is correct.
- 质疑意见：Accurate. The Diamond Sutra colophon is dated 咸通九年四月十五日 (11 May 868), and the British Library calls it the earliest dated printed book. Bi Sheng's clay type in the 庆历 era (1041–1048) is recorded in 《梦溪笔谈》卷十八, and no type survives. The first Gutenberg Bibles were finished in 1454 or 1455. 49 copies are known, but only about 21 are complete (some counts say 25), so adding '含残本' would remove any ambiguity. The ISTC lists about 30,000 incunabula editions. '古腾堡' is a common transliteration; mainland reference works also use 谷登堡 or 古登堡.

## B1（初判：correct；质疑后维持）
- 稳妥措辞：1804年，雅卡尔织机用串联的打孔卡控制织纹。霍尔瑞斯自述，其灵感来自列车员在车票上打孔记录乘客特征。
- 核查发现：Jacquard loom: Wikipedia's Punched card article (citing Essinger, Jacquard's Web, 2007) says that in 1804 Joseph Marie Jacquard demonstrated a loom mechanism driven by punched cards linked into a chain of any length. The Science Museum Group dates the loom's first development to 1804-1805. Some sources give 1801, the year Jacquard showed an earlier mechanism at the Paris industrial exhibition, so '1804' is the standard date. IBM's own history page also names Jacquard's punched-card loom as a precursor. Hollerith's inspiration: in his own recollection (quoted in Austrian's 1982 biography, via Wikipedia) the idea came from a railway 'punch photograph' ticket. The conductor punched a description of the passenger into the ticket, and Hollerith said he 'only made a punch photograph of each person.' The Jacquard link reached him second-hand. MacTutor reports that John Shaw Billings suggested a method 'on the principle of the Jacquard loom,' and that Hollerith studied the loom but found its function 'too far removed' from census work, though he saw that punched cards were an efficient way to store data. ETHW also lists telegraph equipment and library index cards as influences. Conclusion: both claims hold up. Only the train ticket is attested in Hollerith's own words; Jacquard is a precedent cited by others.
- 质疑意见：No error found. Wikipedia's Jacquard machine article dates the patent to 1804. Its Punched card article (citing Essinger) says that in 1804 Jacquard demonstrated a loom mechanism with cards 'linked into a chain of any length', so '串联的打孔卡' is accurate. The ticket story is in Hollerith's own words, quoted in Austrian 1982, p.15: a conductor punched the passenger's traits into a 'punch photograph' ticket. MacTutor agrees. So '自述' is the right label. Minor caveats that do not make the wording wrong: some sources date Jacquard's first showing to 1801. Bouchon and Falcon used punched-card looms earlier, but the wording does not claim Jacquard was first.

## B2（初判：correct；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：据IBM记载，1880年人口普查耗时八年多；1890年借助霍尔瑞斯的打孔卡制表机，六个月完成计数，两年完成全部数据整理。1911年，霍尔瑞斯的制表机公司与其他公司合并组成CTR，该公司于1924年更名为IBM。
- 核查发现：IBM's official history (ibm.com/history/punched-card-tabulator) says 'the 1880 census took more than eight years to complete.' It says the 1890 census with Hollerith's tabulators 'completed the count in six months and finished detailing all the data in two years.' Other sources disagree. Some give 7-9 years for 1880 by hand. For 1890, figures run from a rough count released after about six weeks (made without punched cards), to about three months of counting (MacTutor), to Wikipedia's claim that full processing still took about six years. Present the 2-year figure as IBM's account, not a settled fact. Census Bureau: in the 1888 test on 1880 St. Louis data, Hollerith took 72.5 hours for data capture and 5.5 hours for tabulation prep, far faster than his rivals. Company lineage, per IBM: Hollerith founded the Tabulating Machine Company in 1896. In 1911 Charles Flint bought it and merged it with two other companies into the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company (CTR). CTR was renamed International Business Machines (IBM) in 1924. The claim's lineage is correct, with TMC (1896) as the step before CTR.
- 质疑意见：Two wording problems and one framing risk. (1) IBM's page says the 1880 census 'took more than eight years', but the wording says '约八年' (about eight years), which understates IBM's own figure. (2) IBM splits 1890 into 'completed the count in six months' and 'finished detailing all the data in two years'. Other sources give very different figures. Wikipedia's 1890 census article says processing went 'from eight years for the 1880 census to six years for the 1890 census'. MacTutor says counting took about three months. A rough count came out after six weeks. The 2-year figure is IBM's own company account, and the wording must keep the attribution and the split. (3) '并入CTR' implies CTR already existed. In fact Flint merged Hollerith's Tabulating Machine Company (founded 1896) with other firms in 1911 to create CTR, so '合并组成' is more precise. The 1924 rename to IBM is correct.

## B3（初判：needs_correction；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：1890年人口普查的原始表格在1921年火灾中大量受损，其余受损表格于1930年代中期经国会批准被销毁；如今普通人口调查表仅存6160余人的记录。
- 核查发现：Saying the schedules were 'destroyed after a 1921 fire' is too simple. NARA says a fire on January 10, 1921, in the Commerce Department building in Washington, DC, 'resulted in the destruction of most of the 1890 census.' The fire started around 5 p.m. NARA's Prologue magazine (Kellee Blake, 'First in the Path of the Firemen,' Spring 1996) gives Census Director Sam Rogers's estimate at the time: about 25% destroyed, and 50% of the remainder damaged by water, smoke and fire. The schedules had been stacked outside the vault. The damaged survivors were not lost to the fire alone. The Census Bureau listed them as no longer needed, Congress authorized their destruction on February 21, 1933, and they were destroyed in 1934 or 1935 (sources differ). Fragments found later were accessioned by the National Archives in 1942 and 1953 (microfilm M407, index M496). NARA says 'over 6,160 persons' appear in the surviving general population schedules, and Prologue says 'no fewer than 6,160 names.' The fragments come from 10 states and the District of Columbia. The 1890 census had counted 62,979,766 people. The '~6,000 people' figure is right; use 'about 6,160'.
- 质疑意见：The wording is ambiguous and leaves out a qualifier. (1) The 6,160 figure covers only the surviving GENERAL population schedules. NARA says the 1890 special schedules of Union Civil War veterans and widows also survive for many states (microfilm M123, 118 rolls). So '现仅存约6160人的记录' as a blanket statement for the whole 1890 census is wrong. (2) NARA says 'over 6,160 persons' and Prologue says 'no fewer than 6,160 names', so '6160余人' fits better than '约6160人'. (3) '残件于1930年代被销毁' reads as if the fragments themselves were destroyed, which clashes with the fragments that survive today. What happened: the 1921 fire destroyed about 25% (Rogers's estimate) and damaged much of the rest. Congress authorized destruction of the damaged schedules on 21 Feb 1933, and they were destroyed in 1934 or 1935. Saying 'damaged in the fire' is fine, but the later destruction was a government decision, not the fire.

## C8（初判：correct；质疑后维持）

- 核查发现：The earliest surviving paper is Western Han. The Fangmatan (Tianshui, Gansu) tomb 5 paper fragment with a drawn map is dated c. 179–141 BCE (early 2nd c. BCE); it is a drawing, not text. ## C9（初判：needs_correction；质疑后修订）
- 稳妥措辞：写有文字的泥板已保存五千多年，莎草纸文书约四千五百年，甲骨三千二百余年，竹简两千四百余年。
- 核查发现：Main problem: the counter mixes two kinds of number. The first six are ages that real objects have actually survived. Tape, floppy and HDD are rated or expected lifetimes. Label the last three as 预期寿命, or the counter misleads: the oldest surviving tape recording, Beecham/LPO at BASF Ludwigshafen, Nov 1936, is about 90 years old, and early floppies (1970s) still exist. Item by item: Clay tablets: proto-cuneiform tablets c. 3350–3000 BCE (Uruk IV c. 3350–3200 BCE, CDLI), so 5,000+ years is OK. Papyrus: Diary of Merer, year 26/27 of Khufu, c. 2560 BCE (Guinness: probably c. 2570 BCE); about 4,500 years is OK. Oracle bones: inscribed divination starts in Wu Ding's reign, c. 1250 BCE, about 3,270 years; 3,200+ is OK with 约. Bamboo slips: 曾侯乙墓 slips, burial 433 BCE or slightly later (Xinhua 2025: earliest bamboo slips found, 距今2400多年). Prefer 2,400多年 to 2,450, which is too precise. Diamond Sutra: 868 CE (British Library: earliest complete survival of a dated printed book), 1,158 years; 1,150+ is OK. Punched cards: UNCERTAIN. Jacquard's card-controlled loom dates from 1801–1804 (Falcon's cards from c. 1728), but no source names a dated oldest surviving card; 19th-century Jacquard cards exist in museums. Use 约200年 only as an estimate, or use Hollerith 1890 census cards (about 135 years). Tape: LTO cartridges carry a manufacturer-rated archival life of up to 30 years under proper storage; CCI gives analog tapes 10–30 years and digital tapes 5–10. Write 最长约30年 or 约10至30年. Floppy 5–15 years and HDD 2–5 years match CCI (standard storage conditions). Short second sentence for the modern counter: 预期寿命：磁带最长约30年，软盘5至15年，硬盘2至5年。
- 质疑意见：The papyrus figure is ambiguous as written. The oldest surviving papyrus is a blank roll from Hemaka's tomb at Saqqara (1st Dynasty, c. 2900 BCE), about 4,900 years old. About 4,500 years (Diary of Merer, c. 2560 BCE) is only correct for the oldest papyrus with writing on it. A viewer who looks up 'oldest papyrus' will find about 5,000 years. The other figures hold. Proto-cuneiform tablets date to c. 3350–3000 BCE (5,000+ years). Wu Ding-era oracle bones date to c. 1250 BCE (about 3,250 years, so 三千二百余年 is OK). The Zeng Hou Yi bamboo slips are still described as the earliest found (Xinhua, Oct 2025: 距今2400多年). Specifying 'with writing' (有字/文书) keeps the four items consistent.

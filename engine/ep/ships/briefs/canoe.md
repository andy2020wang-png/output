# Brief: canoe

## Style card
Coloured, photographic dawn — NOT ink wash, NOT painterly, no paper texture or brush edges (episode 1 already did ink wash). Pre-dawn blue-violet sky shifting to a peach horizon during the 10 s (dawn breaks within the shot). Atmospheric depth by hue shifts (cool distant layers), mist bands, water with reflections and ripple distortion, soft light shafts as the sun nears. Begin darker (the hook→canoe join must stay low in luminance) and brighten over the first 2 beats.

## The one shot
Low, water-level lateral tracking shot following a single dugout canoe gliding left→right on a calm lake/marsh (Kuahuqiao was a lakeside site). Two paddlers with single-bladed paddles, stroking exactly on beats 1,3,5,…,15 (paddle enters water at each event time; ripples spread from each stroke). On the near shore or a mud bank, a second, half-hollowed pine log with glowing embers and a thin smoke trail (fire-assisted hollowing) — the only warm accent, visible during the card (2.5-9.25 s) which talks about char marks. No outrigger.

## Text
opener(): title 独木舟 + era line, placed in the sky area; subs() bottom centre over calm water with a subtle dark gradient band if needed.

## Next transition (for awareness; built later in a separate phase)
Out: water ripples → painted zigzag water lines of the Egyptian model river.
Keep the last beat (local 9.375-10 s; hook 14.375-15; outro holds to the end) compositionally stable with that anchor element clearly visible.

## Researcher's accuracy notes (follow unless they conflict with the style card above, which wins)
- Hull: one pine log, very long, narrow and SHALLOW. About 5.6 m long, 52 cm at the widest, only about 15 cm deep, sides 2–3 cm thick. Draw a slim, low trough with a length-to-beam ratio of about 11:1 and very low freeboard. Not a deep tub, and not a Western canoe with high sides.
- Ends: the preserved end is FLAT and blunt, 29 cm wide. Do NOT draw upturned, pointed birchbark-canoe ends. Do NOT add a dragon head, even though some Chinese media nickname it a 龙舟.
- Surface: bare, dark, water-soaked pine with darker char patches inside the hull. No paint, carving, characters, eyes or ornaments. No planks, ribs, thwarts, nails or any metal.
- No mast, sail, rudder, oarlocks or flags of any kind. Propulsion is paddling only.
- Paddles: wooden, single-bladed, about 1.4 m long (two were found beside the canoe). The paddler kneels or sits low and faces forward. Do not draw a double-bladed kayak paddle or rowing with oars.
- Outrigger: unproven. Timbers beside the canoe might be outrigger remains, but no attachment survives. The safest choice is a plain single hull. Never draw a Polynesian outrigger with a crab-claw sail.
- Water and setting: calm inland water (lake, marsh or river) in the Lower Yangtze wetlands near the Qiantang estuary, with reeds, low hills (Xianghu) and pines. Antiquity judges the canoe suited to lakes and rivers, not the open sea, so no ocean swell or surf.
- Shore cues that make it read as Kuahuqiao: stilt houses (干栏式) on wooden piles over the marsh, and wooden stakes in the shallows. Optional: a half-hollowed log on the shore with a low ember glow and smoke, and polished stone adzes on L-shaped wooden handles.
- People: Neolithic, wearing plain plant-fibre or hide clothing. No bronze or metal jewellery, no conical bamboo hats, no feather headdresses (a Native American stereotype) and no Polynesian tattoos.
- If the hook's line drawing shows Pesse as the oldest canoe, it should be SHORT (about 3 m) and stubby, with blunt ends, not the long Kuahuqiao shape. Its wheel should be a solid wooden disc made of planks with a square axle hole (Ljubljana type). Spoked wheels are about 1,500 years later and wrong here.
- Pacing: a relaxed paddle stroke every 2 beats (1.25 s at 96 BPM) looks natural. Every beat (96 strokes a minute) looks like frantic racing.

## Researcher's style comments (background)
The 晨雾剪影 style (silhouettes in dawn mist) suits this era: it has no hard anachronism risk and contrasts clearly with the next chapter's warm painted stone relief (彩绘浮雕). The single low lateral tracking shot along the water with parallax mist layers is a good one-shot solution. Main risk: a lone paddler in silhouette is generic and can read as a Native American or Amazonian canoe. Put Kuahuqiao cues into the silhouette layers: a row of stilt houses on piles, reed beds, the low Xianghu hills, the canoe's very low flat profile with a blunt flat end, and a kneeling paddler with a single-blade paddle. Strongly recommended: as the camera tracks, pass a shore spot with a half-hollowed log glowing with embers and a thin smoke ribbon drifting into the mist. This is the only warm accent, and it shows the card's fire fact inside the same continuous shot. Keep a darker band of water in the lower third so the white two-line card stays legible over the bright mist for its 7 s. The transition, where ripples freeze into the Egyptian zigzag water sign of the relief, is strong and fits. Keep the paddle cadence to one stroke per 2 beats.

# Brief: dreadnought

## Style card
Vorticism (Edward Wadsworth, BLAST 1914): the WORLD around the ship — sky, sea, searchlight beams, smoke — rendered as hard-edged angular planes and diagonal force lines in black, off-white, ochre and steel blue. The SHIP itself stays correct battleship grey with crisp flat-shaded facets (HMS Dreadnought had no dazzle scheme). No red, no lettering, no BLAST typography. A calm band behind the subtitle.

## The one shot
A slow orbit around HMS Dreadnought: two tall funnels, tripod foremast, five twin 12-inch turrets (A forward; P and Q wing turrets amidships either side; X and Y aft on the centreline). Turrets train together, eased, beats 7-11; ONE full salvo on beat 13 (7.5 s) — flashes and an angular smoke burst that the Vorticist planes amplify.

## Text
opener(): 无畏舰 in a hard geometric sans (Bebas Neue for Latin feel / Noto Sans SC 700); subs() on the calm band.

## Next transition (for awareness; built later in a separate phase)
Out: the angular force lines straighten into the flight deck's painted lines.
Keep the last beat (local 9.375-10 s; hook 14.375-15; outro holds to the end) compositionally stable with that anchor element clearly visible.

## Researcher's accuracy notes (follow unless they conflict with the style card above, which wins)
- Paint: the whole ship is Admiralty grey: hull, superstructure, turrets, funnels and masts. The Royal Navy dropped the black hull, white upperworks and buff funnels in 1902–03. Decks are pale natural wood planking. Do NOT put dazzle stripes on the ship itself, because there is no record of Dreadnought being dazzle-painted.
- Turrets: five twin 12-inch turrets. A is forward on the centreline. P (port) and Q (starboard) are wing turrets beside the forward superstructure. X and Y are aft on the centreline. All are on one level: NO superfiring turrets and NO triple turrets. This is the most common mistake, copying later Queen Elizabeth or Iowa layouts.
- When the turrets all swing to one side, the wing turret on the far side cannot point across the deck. Only 8 of the 10 guns bear on one side, so show A, X, Y and the near wing turret aimed out, and the far wing turret held fore-and-aft or aimed to its own side.
- Two funnels and a single tripod foremast set right behind (aft of) the forward funnel. The spotting top at its head sits in the funnel smoke, a real, well-known flaw, so let the smoke wrap it. Do not add a second tripod; Bellerophon (1907) introduced two tripods. Aft there was only a light pole mast with a boat derrick; check it against a 1906–07 photo such as IWM Q 21183 or RMG P00030.
- Hull: high forecastle, long low hull, about 160.6 m long and 25 m wide, about 18,000 t. Pre-war she carried anti-torpedo nets on booms along the hull sides; they were removed early in WWI. Keep them only for a 1906–14 look.
- Small guns: 3-inch (12-pounder) guns on turret roofs and the superstructure only. No anti-aircraft guns, radar, aircraft or catapults, and no closed modern bridge. 1906 had an open compass platform and a conning tower.
- Funnel smoke: thick dark brown-grey coal smoke (she burned coal, with oil sprayed in as a supplement). A curling bow wave suits 21 kn.
- Flags: White Ensign at the stern (and at the mastheads in action); a Union Jack on the bow staff only at anchor. AVOID painted hull numbers (pennant numbers on the hull are post-WWI or WWII), funnel identification bands, German or Japanese flags, and the modern naval ensign.
- Crew of about 700 in 1907: tiny figures in navy blue, or white in summer, with flat caps. Keep them as silhouettes at this scale.
- If dazzle must appear, its documented colours were black, white, greys, blues and greens in hard-edged, broken geometric shapes. Use it on the background or in the transition, not on Dreadnought's hull.

## Researcher's style comments (background)
Era fit: Vorticism (BLAST, 1914–15) and dazzle (Royal Navy from Aug 1917) belong to WWI, the peak of the dreadnought era, which is fair for the ship type. They come 8–11 years after HMS Dreadnought herself (1906), and she has no documented dazzle scheme. She stayed grey and was paid off into reserve on 7 Aug 1918. Since the card names "“无畏”号" and the shot shows her five-turret, wing-turret profile, a dazzle-painted Dreadnought would be a mistake that ship-history viewers are likely to point out in the comments.
Recommended fix: keep the Vorticist look for the world around her, not her paint. Draw sky, sea, searchlight beams and smoke as hard-edged angular planes in an Edward Wadsworth-like palette (black, off-white, ochre, steel blue). Render the ship in correct grey with crisp flat-shaded facets, so it reads as a Vorticist picture of a real grey Dreadnought.
Transition: move the dazzle stripes into the background planes and the transition. "Dazzle stripes straighten into flight-deck markings" has a real basis: the dazzle article shows HMS Argus, the first carrier with a full-length flat deck, in dazzle in 1918. This hands off cleanly to the carrier shot.
Distinctness: hard-edged geometric colour clearly differs from the silver collodion of the previous ironclad shot and the softer WWII gouache poster of the next, so it stays visually distinct.
Single shot: limit the orbit to about 90–120° of yaw over the 10 s, starting bow-quarter and ending broadside. This shows the five-turret layout without a dizzying full circle and gives a stable broadside frame while the card is up (about 2.5–10 s). Busy Vorticist backgrounds hurt legibility, so put a calm, low-contrast sky or sea band behind the subtitle.
Turrets: the beat-synced turret moves should be slow and heavy, with eased moves of about 15–25° per beat or one continuous sweep, not snap-turns. Respect the wing-turret firing arcs.
Guns: if they fire, use one restrained broadside at the climax, rendered as soft smoke blooms rather than flashes, to stay within the no-harsh-sound rule.

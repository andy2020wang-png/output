# Brief: ironclad

## Style card
Wet-plate collodion "living tintype" (stylised, not documentary — no photos of the battle exist): deep blacks, warm brown-black tint, silvery highlight sheen, dark oval vignette, collodion pour edges and faint chemical streaks, long-exposure softness on smoke and water (smear, not crisp). Keep a clean, readable mid-ground.

## The one shot
Slow push toward USS Monitor-type ironclad: a low flat deck nearly awash, a single round revolving turret with two guns, a small square pilot house forward. The turret starts rotating at beat 3 (1.25 s) for ~6 s. A distant gun flash at beat 9 (5.0 s); at beat 11 (6.25 s) a shell glances off the turret armour (a dent + a puff + small deflected debris, no sparks shower); a smaller glance at beat 13.5. The CSS Virginia (casemate ironclad, sloped iron roof) may appear as a dim shape in the haze.

## Text
opener(): 铁甲舰 in a period wood-type style (Rye/Smokum are too western-saloon; prefer Playfair/IM Fell + Noto Serif SC); subs() on a dark band integrated with the plate edge.

## Next transition (for awareness; built later in a separate phase)
Out: the turret's rotation sweep line cuts in angular Vorticist planes.
Keep the last beat (local 9.375-10 s; hook 14.375-15; outro holds to the end) compositionally stable with that anchor element clearly visible.

## Researcher's accuracy notes (follow unless they conflict with the style card above, which wins)
- USS Monitor hull: a very low, flat 'raft'. The armoured deck stood only about 18 in (46 cm) above the water and was awash in any swell. The upper armoured hull overhangs a narrower lower hull. Length 179 ft (54.6 m), beam 41.5 ft. Critics called her a 'cheesebox on a raft'.
- Turret: one squat cylinder amidships, 20 ft (6.1 m) across and 9 ft (2.7 m) high, built from stacked layers of 1-inch wrought iron, 8 in thick (11 in at the ports). Show rows of bolt or rivet heads, a flat roof of iron grating, and NO hood, rangefinder or long barrels.
- Gunports: two side by side, carrying two 11-inch Dahlgren smoothbores. These are fat 'soda-bottle' guns that fired from just inside the ports; do not draw long protruding barrels. Heavy pendulum iron port stoppers swing shut between shots.
- Turret rotation: driven by steam donkey engines through gears, a full turn in about 22.5 s in testing (about 160 degrees in a 10 s shot). It had no brake, so in the battle it was kept turning and fired as the ports swept past the enemy (Greene: 'the whirligig motion of the tower'). Keep the rotation slow and continuous; never snap it into aim.
- Pilothouse: a small iron box with viewing slits on the deck near the bow, with UPRIGHT sides in March 1862. The sloped 30-degree pilothouse came after the battle. It was hit, and Worden was blinded.
- No masts, sails or rigging in battle, and no tall funnel. Only low deck fittings; the 30-ft smokestack was added in October 1862. The deck should be empty, because all 49 crew were inside.
- Battle damage: the turret took 9 of Monitor's 22 hits and shows shallow round dents with no holes. Dents appearing one per beat would be a strong, accurate visual.
- Projectile physics: Virginia fired ONLY explosive shell, so shells hitting Monitor's turret should burst into fragments and smoke and leave dents, not bounce off whole. The 'bouncing ball' look fits only Monitor's solid 11-inch cast-iron shot glancing off Virginia's sloped casemate.
- CSS Virginia (if seen behind Monitor): about 275 ft, nearly twice Monitor's size. Her bow and stern decks sat awash, with a long central casemate sloped at 36 degrees, rounded ends and two crossed layers of 2-inch iron plates on about 24 in of oak and pine. One large funnel already riddled with holes. No ram, which broke off in USS Cumberland on 8 March. 14 gunports, with shutters only at bow and stern; about 320 crew.
- Setting: Hampton Roads by day, morning to midday, flat estuary water, heavy low black coal smoke and white powder smoke. The ships fought from a few yards to about 100 yards apart, so frame them close together. A grounded three-masted wooden steam frigate (USS Minnesota) can sit in the background.
- Flags to AVOID: any 50- or 48-star US flag (the correct flag would have 34 stars), and above all the Confederate 'Southern Cross' battle flag. It was not the navy jack in March 1862 and it is politically sensitive for the audience. Safest is to show no flags on either ship.
- Avoid these anachronisms: steel finish, hull numbers, camouflage, cage or tripod masts, breech-loading guns with long barrels, searchlights, radio aerials, smokeless muzzle flashes, and a tall funnel on Monitor.
- Era alternative: if the era line keeps Gloire and Warrior, Gloire was a wooden-hulled, barquentine-rigged broadside frigate. Warrior was a long black three-masted ship-rigged frigate with two telescopic funnels and a figurehead. Do not merge either with the turret ship.

## Researcher's style comments (background)
Wet-plate collodion and tintype suit this ship well. The collodion process dates from 1851 and the tintype from 1856. The Civil War was the first war photographed widely this way, and James F. Gibson photographed Monitor's crew and dented turret on the James River in July 1862 (Library of Congress), which gives a real visual reference. It is also clearly different from the hand-coloured engraving before it and the colourful Vorticism after it.

**Risks:**
1. **No photographs of the battle exist.** Exposures took seconds, so present this as a stylised "living tintype", not documentary footage. Smoke and moving water should smear softly the way long exposures do.
2. **Low contrast on a phone.** Silver-grey on silver-grey turns murky. Push deep blacks, a warm brown-black tint with a silvery highlight sheen, a dark oval vignette, collodion pour edges and faint chemical streaks. Keep a clean dark band behind the subtitle.
3. **Muzzle flashes:** render them as blown-out white blooms (halation), not coloured fire, to stay true to the medium.
4. **"炮弹在装甲上弹开" is only half accurate.** Virginia's shells should burst against Monitor's turret and leave dents. Glancing bounces belong to Monitor's solid shot hitting Virginia's slope.

**Suggested single shot:** a slow push-in at near water level toward the turret, with Virginia's sloped casemate looming in haze behind it. The turret rotates continuously, a port swings past and fires on the downbeat, and incoming shells burst on the turret, each leaving a new dent on the beat. The exit transition still works: the turret's sweep line carries into the geometric colour blocks of the Vorticist dreadnought.

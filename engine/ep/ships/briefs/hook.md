# Brief: hook

## Style card
Archaeological find-drawing on warm off-black: matte, pale brass-gold line work (#C9A86A-ish on #15120E), no bloom, no glow. Real drawing conventions: plan, profile and cross-section views, stipple shading, hatching for cut surfaces, a scale bar with ticks but NO numbers. Paper-like grain on the dark ground. Two objects drawn as in an excavation report: a generic dugout canoe (exactly the silhouette the canoe shot uses: one hollowed pine log, rounded bow, low sides, single-bladed paddle beside it) and a solid disc wheel (two or three planks joined by cross-battens, square axle hole, NO spokes, no metal rim).

## Researcher's accuracy notes (follow unless they conflict with the style card above, which wins)
- Draw the hook canoe as exactly the canoe that chapter 1 uses, so the planned transition (line art filling with dawn light) carries over cleanly. That is the Kuahuqiao type: one hollowed 马尾松 log, about 5.6 m surviving length, about 52 cm at its widest, walls only 2–3 cm thick, low sides, a plain rounded bow, and a cross-timber found under the bow. If the reveal names the oldest canoe (alt cards), the Pesse boat is shorter (2.98 m by 0.44 m, Scots pine) but has the same plain dugout shape.
- Dugout canoe must have: no planks, nails, ribs, keel, mast, sail, rudder, oarlocks or benches. Show adze or gouge marks on the inside, and if you like, darker charred patches where fire was used to soften the wood. The crew uses single-bladed wooden paddles (two were found with the Kuahuqiao canoe). Never draw double-bladed kayak paddles or oars.
- Leave out the outrigger in the hook. The Kuahuqiao outrigger is only a hypothesis tested in a 2015 replica, and an outrigger also makes the canoe read as Polynesian.
- The wheel must be a SOLID plank disc. Ljubljana type: two ash planks joined by cross-battens, about 70 cm across and 5 cm thick, square axle hole, oak axle turning with the wheel. Mesopotamian disc wheels are three planks held by battens. NO SPOKES: spoked wheels date from about 2000 BC (Sintashta). Also no metal rim, no round bearing hub, no ship's steering wheel (舵轮), no cart or chariot.
- If you show the vehicle at all, a single axle with two disc wheels, or a simple four-wheeled box wagon as on the Bronocice pot, is correct. Horses are wrong for this date: early wagons were pulled by oxen.
- If the Australia reveal is used and you add a faint map contour behind the canoe, draw Ice Age coastlines. Sahul is one landmass (Australia, New Guinea and Tasmania joined, with no Torres or Bass Strait). Sunda is mainland Southeast Asia joined to Sumatra, Java and Borneo. Wallacea (Sulawesi, Lesser Sundas, Timor) stays as separate islands with open water on both sides. Do not draw a modern map of Australia.
- Avoid any text inside the art (no labels, no dates on the drawing, no small corner captions), and keep the series label and the 'A 船 / B 车轮' options at full caption size.
- Avoid symbols that are wrong for the period: no Egyptian, Viking or Polynesian decoration on the canoe, and no pictograms as decoration.

## Researcher's style comments (background)
The 黑底金线考古线描 card suits the hook. It does not belong to any one era, it reads as evidence, and it contrasts well with the chapter 1 晨雾剪影 card it turns into. The one shot (canoe and wheel side by side, the wheel fading on the reveal, the title on the drop of 八拍 3) is clear and stays continuous.

Risks and improvements:
(1) Gold-on-black is the look of countless generic 'premium' templates. To make it distinctive and true to the subject, draw like a real archaeological find drawing: top view plus profile plus cross-section, stippled shading, hatching for tool marks, and a plain scale bar with no numbers. Let the line draw itself on the beat.
(2) The opening seconds decide retention on Douyin. Show the question in full and large on beat 1 of 八拍 1, with no slow build-up.
(3) The hook runs 3 八拍 (15 s), while every ship runs 2. That fits the plan, but the reveal card gets only 5 s, so keep it at 34 characters or fewer (the best card is 34).
(4) With the Australia reveal, a faint gold Ice Age contour of the Sunda–Sahul islands can trace in behind the canoe while the wheel fades. This stays one continuous shot and matches the text visually. Keep it subtle, below the subtitle's contrast.
(5) The canoe in the hook should be geometrically identical to the chapter 1 canoe, so the dawn fill-in transition is a true match cut.
(6) Avoid a quiz-show feel (a flashing green tick or a red cross). Mark the answer by having the canoe's line fill with warm gold while the wheel line dims to grey.
